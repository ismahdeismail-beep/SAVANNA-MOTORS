Savanna Motors — identity options

Three symbolic marks. No initials, no generic car silhouette, no chrome. Flat, two-colour, and drawn in the palette the site already uses: Savanna green with a single Acacia gold.

Each mark is shown at real size for a browser tab (16), a favicon on a retina display (32), the site header (40) and a showroom sign (96), on both the light and dark surfaces it will live on. Judge the 16 and 32 rows first — that is where most logos fail.

Concept A — Acacia implemented

The umbrella acacia is the one silhouette that says savanna without a caption, and the company already owns the name. Flat-topped canopy, thin forked trunk, and a hairline of road running under it so the tree stands beside the journey rather than on a dead horizon. It cannot be mistaken for a manufacturer badge, which matters when it sits in the window next to Toyota and Subaru crests.

Where it comes from

2026 automotive branding has flattened out — no chrome, no bevels, and marks that have to read as a 16 px tab icon. The counter-trend is character over sterility, and the strongest dealership marks lean on something local rather than on a generic car outline or a monogram. For a Kenyan importer named Savanna, the flat-topped acacia is the honest answer: it is the country's own silhouette, it is not on anyone's grille, and it belongs to this company and no other.
